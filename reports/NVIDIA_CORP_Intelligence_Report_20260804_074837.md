## Executive Summary

NVIDIA CORP closed FY2026 with $215,938M of revenue, up 65.5% year over year, and $120,067M of net income (55.6% net margin).

On a trailing-twelve-month basis through 2026-04-26 the company generated $253,491M of revenue and $159,613M of net income — a 63.0% net margin.

Ten findings drive this report's conclusions.

**One.** Revenue grew +65.5% in FY2026 to $215,938M, at a 55.6% net margin — holding against 55.9% a year earlier. Growth is decelerating — the prior year grew +114.2%, so the rate has moved -48.7 points.

**Two.** The discounted cash flow model values the equity at $77.51 against a traded $206.64, a gap of -62%. The model is not asserting mispricing so much as measuring the growth the market is underwriting; the Valuation section inverts it to state that rate explicitly.

**Four.** The latest proxies disclose 13 related-party passages under Item 404, the largest at $108.3M. Arrangements with family members of officers and directors, and transactions with entities they control, are the conflicts the financial statements do not surface on their own.

**Five.** Private-company holdings carried under the measurement alternative closed the year at $22.25B, of which $17.44B was capital deployed during the year rather than revaluation — 78% of the book is current deployment.

**Six.** 5 outside public-company seats are currently held by this board's directors and officers, read from their own Section 16 filings.

## Valuation

The valuation is a discounted cash flow on unlevered free cash flow, run over an explicit 10-year forecast with a Gordon terminal value. Every input is derived from filed figures or quoted market data; none is hand-set for this issuer, which is what makes the output comparable across names.

### The free cash flow base

Free cash flow is operating cash flow less capital expenditure, both taken from SEC XBRL company facts and matched on period end date rather than filing year, so a restated or late-filed period cannot pair the wrong two figures together.

| Fiscal year | Period end | Operating cash flow | CapEx | Free cash flow |
|-------------|------------|---------------------|-------|----------------|
| FY2026 | 2026-01-25 | $102,718M | $6,042M | $96,676M |
| FY2025 | 2025-01-26 | $64,089M | $3,236M | $60,853M |
| FY2024 | 2024-01-28 | $28,090M | $1,069M | $27,021M |

The model starts from FY2026 free cash flow of $96,676M. Realised growth across the retrieved history compounds at 89.2% a year. The forecast does not extend that rate: it opens at 25.0% and fades linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate by year 10, because an 89% compound sustained for a decade implies an end-state larger than any plausible addressable market.

### Discount rate

Cost of equity is CAPM on a 4.75% risk-free rate taken from the 10-year Treasury, and a raw equity beta of 2.21, pulled toward the market by the standard Blume adjustment to 1.810. That yields 14.71%. After-tax cost of debt is 4.94%. At a capital structure of 99.7% equity and 0.3% debt, the weighted average cost of capital is 14.68%.

The beta is the single most consequential input here. A raw 2.21 reflects realised volatility far above the market, and it drives the discount rate well above what a cash-generative balance sheet would otherwise carry. The Blume adjustment tempers it on the reasoning that betas mean-revert, but a reader who believes the volatility is transitory should read the lower-WACC column of the grid below as the more relevant one.

### What the market price implies

Inverting the model answers the more useful question. Holding the 14.68% discount rate and 2.5% terminal growth fixed, the traded price of $206.64 is consistent with opening free cash flow growth of 54.6%, fading on the same schedule — against the 25.0% the base case assumes.

So the 62% gap between the model and the market is not a claim that the company is worth less than it trades for. It is a statement that buyers are underwriting roughly 55% near-term cash flow growth where this model underwrites 25%. Whether that is optimism or foresight is a judgement about demand durability, not about arithmetic.

### Do discretionary insider sales precede weakness?

A 10b5-1 sale is scheduled months ahead, so its date says nothing about what the seller knew that week. A discretionary sale is chosen. Comparing the two holds the issuer, the period and the people constant, which is what removes the market drift that would otherwise have to be modelled.

| Sale type | Dates | Mean 30-day return after |
|-----------|------:|-------------------------------:|
| Discretionary | 18 | +4.68% |
| 10b5-1 plan | 71 | +2.77% |

The discretionary mean carries a 95% interval of +0.83% to +8.53% (p=0.0171 against no move).

**No relationship.** The gap between discretionary and plan sales is +1.91 percentage points at p=0.3686, which is indistinguishable from zero on this sample. Discretionary sellers at NVIDIA CORP are not, on this evidence, timing their sales any better than the calendar does. That is a result worth stating: the opposite would have been reported here in the same terms.

### Reporting conventions

- Figures are US GAAP in millions of USD unless otherwise labelled.
- Quarters marked DERIVED are computed as the fiscal year less Q1–Q3.
- Values that could not be retrieved are shown as `—` and are never estimated.
- Fiscal years follow the issuer's own calendar, which for many companies does not align with the calendar year.
- Where a figure is quoted from a filing, the filing rather than a data vendor is the cited source.
- Charts are drawn only from fields already tabulated in the report; a missing series omits the figure rather than inventing a shape.
- Network findings name a person or entity only when a filed document places them on both sides of the comparison.
- Statistical correlations, when present, state sample size n and are suppressed entirely where n < 12.

### Quality gates

The report is checked before it is rendered, and the build fails rather than emitting a document that would read as complete while carrying unresolved values. The gates are:

- Placeholder and sentinel strings are rejected, so a template fragment cannot reach the page as if it were data. Embedded chart payloads are excluded from that scan so base64 content cannot false-trigger.
- A minimum populated section count, so a run in which most connectors failed does not ship as a thin but valid report.
- A minimum word count, which catches a run that populated its section headers but not their contents.
- The subject company must be named, which catches a misrouted ticker resolving to the wrong filer.
- Sections without supporting data are omitted, never filled with industry boilerplate presented as issuer-specific fact.
